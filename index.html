1 The Toy Cipher

This is a tiny 16-bit cipher with 4 rounds — small enough to see every step. In each round we XOR a key, run four S-boxes, then mix the bits (except the last round). At the end we XOR one last key, K5. The S-box comes from the first row of DES S-box 1.

S-box: 0→E 1→4 2→D 3→1 4→2 5→F 6→B 7→8 8→3 9→A A→6 B→C C→5 D→9 E→0 F→7

2 Difference Distribution Table

Think of this table as a scoreboard for the S-box. For each input difference ΔX and output difference ΔY, we count how often S(x) ⊕ S(x ⊕ ΔX) = ΔY happens (out of 16). Divide by 16 and you get the probability.

The green cells are the ones we care about for our attack: B→2 (8/16) 4→6 (6/16) 2→5 (6/16)

3 Differential Characteristic

Here is the path we follow through the cipher. We only need it to work often enough — then we can peel off 8 bits of the last key, K5.

ΔP
0x0B00
S12
B → 2 (8/16)
S23
4 → 6 (6/16)
S32 + S33
2 → 5 (6/16 each)
Expected ΔU4
…6…6…

Chance this whole path holds: pD = 27/1024 ≈ 2.64%. Not huge — but with a few thousand pairs, it is enough.

5 Differential Attack

We pick plaintext pairs that differ by 0x0B00, encrypt them, and score every possible 8-bit guess for part of K5. The right guess should rise to the top.
